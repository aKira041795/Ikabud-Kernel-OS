WEDGE:
  position:      Akira should contend only on **audit-grade change control for public content that already carries a compliance obligation** — provable, exportable authority over who approved what — and should stop calling itself a general CMS contender.
  why_unoccupied: WordPress cannot enforce segregation of duties without replacing its role/extension model, its audit trail is operator-controlled and plugin-level, and its free-core/volume economics make compliance-grade proof a low-margin, off-segment custom build [I]. Contentful/Sanity/Strapi/Payload/Directus sell developer velocity to product teams; their audit logs are a settings feature and their buyers don't ask for a third-party-verifiable export, so there is no product pull [I]. Enterprise WCM (Adobe/Sitecore/Optimizely) does occupy the compliance cell — but at enterprise price and procurement weight. The unoccupied cell is *audit-grade, self-hosted, SMB-affordable*, and it is unoccupied mainly because it is small and unglamorous, not because it is hard [I].
  buyer:         A regulated or contractually-obliged small organisation — clinic, local government unit, school, cooperative/franchise — that must show who approved a public claim and what it said at that time. Today it runs WordPress + a review spreadsheet or email chain, or pays an agency that keeps its own private audit trail. It buys because an auditor or a contract asks, not because it wants a better CMS [I — I found no such buyer anywhere in this repo].
  confidence:    low. Raise it with one paid pilot or signed LOI, or by demonstrating that a real auditor requirement is not satisfied by WordPress + a history/audit plugin. I want to be blunt: on the evidence in this repository I do not believe Akira has a *CMS* wedge a small team on Bluehost shared hosting can win. The only wedge actually present in the repo is the **kernel as an embedded authority layer**, and its proof is `daily-ledger` (a live money domain: 119 route entries, 31 DiSyL admin/cashier templates [M]), not Akira (15 modules, **0** `{ikb_entity_view}` contracts of its own, every module `_enabled:false` [M]). If effort must earn money, it should go to a vertical the kernel already underwrites, not to the CMS category.

SEQUENCE:                       # exactly three
  1. Governed Media Library (the missing usable surface)
     claim:      a fully governed write driven from the UI: a POST route declares `akira.media.upload@1` in `capabilities.routes` and is enforced at dispatch before the handler runs, is idempotent and audited in one tenant transaction, and the list is rendered through a registered `{ikb_entity_view}` contract — i.e. governance is visible *in* the screen, not beside it.
     value:      upload an image, set alt/caption, insert it into a post, and delete it with confirmation. This is the single biggest reason a real article cannot be published in Akira today ([M]: `cms-akira-media` has 6 full CRUD capabilities and 713 lines of backend helpers but 0 declared route authorities and 0 admin surface).
     order_why:  cheapest surface that produces a genuinely usable product, and it requires no new primitive — the expensive part (capabilities, tables, tests) already exists [M]. It also establishes the one admin pattern (entity-view list + declared write routes) that every later screen copies; doing P3 first would put the differentiator on a product nobody can use. It also directly attacks the census debt rather than growing it.
     smallest:   one `/cms-akira-shell/media` GET list, one POST upload route declaring `akira.media.upload@1`, one POST delete route declaring `akira.media.delete@1`, one `media/list` entity-view contract registered under a `helpers/views/` layer that today contains exactly one contract (daily-ledger's) [M], and an "insert into post" button appending the stream URL. Single image per upload; no thumbnails, folders, crop, or image editing. Estimate [I]: 1–2 PRs, days.

  2. Delegation, minimal (P3) with approval as a governed transition
     claim:      delegated authority is a runtime property enforced at the bus: `Actor`/`Grant`/`ExecutionContext`, a grant that narrows and expires, and segregation of duties (the approving actor must differ from the acting actor) enforced as a capability decision, not a setting.
     value:      an operator issues a scoped grant to a service/cron/agent ("may create drafts, may not publish, expires in 7 days"); a human holding *different* authority approves; both appear in provenance — demonstrated on Akira publish **and on one money operation in `daily-ledger`** (e.g. a void/reopen), because the doc's own bar is two materially different domains and `daily-ledger` is the only live domain [M].
     order_why:  it is the only claim incumbents demonstrably cannot match, and it is the prerequisite for both the AI product claim and P4 (a proof must bind a grant). It must come after #1 because a bound differentiator on an unusable product is unsellable, and after the 5 remaining Akira route declarations because a grant over undeclared routes is theatre.
     smallest:   reuse `transitionGrantState()` (the only, already-audited state mover [M]) and the existing `AuthorityScope`; add `Actor{human|service|machine}` and `ExecutionContext` to the capability call, a `grant:issue` CLI, one admin list screen, and a `require_distinct_actor` constraint on one workflow transition. No new substrate primitive is justified here. Scope explicitly to **one** `daily-ledger` operation, not its 52 writes [M] — governing all 52 is a programme, not an iteration.

  3. Proof export (P4) for one entity and one transition
     claim:      an exported artifact (`artifact.json` + `proof.json` + public key) binds content hash + actor + grant + policy version + provenance chain and verifies on a machine that never talks to the server.
     value:      a "prove this revision" button and a standalone `verify-artifact` CLI a third party runs; this is the compliance claim that becomes sellable.
     order_why:  it converts authority from an internal property into a portable claim a buyer can hand an auditor, and its value depends on #2 because the signature must bind a *grant*, not just a user id. Current revision UI shows only `actor_user_id` ("User #N"), action and timestamp [M] — no capability, grant or policy version — so this is not a repackaging job.
     smallest:   Ed25519 signature over canonical JSON of one post revision's hash + actor + policy version + grant id + prior revision hash; per-tenant key in tenant settings; `verify-artifact.php`; one page of verification docs. Explicitly no blockchain/PKI/DID. Flag: this needs a **new signing primitive** — `kernel/Crypto.php` is symmetric encryption only, with no sign/verify path [M] — so it is the one place a new primitive is justified.

GATED_AI:
  verdict:        demo
  argument:       Measured, not inferred: `cms-akira-ai` is not AI. Its manifest says "deterministic local summary and keyword suggestions", it has no provider SDK and no remote transport, and it ships `_enabled:false` [M]. The real AI seam in `kernel/DiSyL/AI/` defaults to `EchoAiProvider`, which returns `[ai:MODEL] <the prompt>` [M]; `Policy` is constructed per render with an in-memory cost accumulator and its own comment says the per-tenant DB-backed ceiling is future work [M]; the review queue, AI audit trail and provider config are JSON files under `storage/`, not the capability bus or kernel audit [M]; the AI admin endpoints are gated by `kernelRequireSuperadmin()` — a role check on a kernel-scoped surface, which per measured fact #3 has **no authority store at all** [M]; and `ikb_ai_assist`'s "human approval" is a `review='required'` attribute rendering a "Draft — requires review" badge, with a comment admitting the interactive version is a placeholder [M]. "Schedule" appears nowhere in the repo [M]. So the direction doc's line "Akira already holds every primitive needed … it costs no new substrate" is false as implemented: AI governance here is a parallel, file-based, role-gated stack — the exact "reimplemented it locally" anti-pattern that P2 was written to condemn in `daily-ledger`. Would a buyer pay? No, for two reasons: every CMS already prevents a user/agent from publishing via ordinary role permissions, so "an AI may not publish" is not a differentiator on its own; and the drafting itself is commoditised. What *would* be sellable is the grant-bound receipt and off-server proof — which is #2+#3 and does not exist. What would break my verdict: a real provider behind a declared capability, a per-tenant cost ceiling persisted in the DB, and an agent approval implemented as a capability transition requiring a different grant, demonstrated end-to-end with an export. What would reduce it further to a conference demo: another `EchoAiProvider` path, another badge, another `storage/*.json` queue.
  cheapest_test:  Two options. Cheapest (no code): stop asserting the AI claim in any pitch, and put the current build in front of 5 target buyers, offering only the proof/audit story against their incumbent (WordPress + a history plugin). If none will pay or pilot, the AI thesis is moot and the "gated" claim was never the purchase reason. Slightly dearer (~1–2 days): wire a real provider behind a declared capability with a persisted ceiling and re-run the same 5 conversations. I would run the cheap one first — it costs days and answers the question the code cannot.

DO_NOT_BUILD:
  - **ARK Theme Studio** (doc item #4). A visual theming/canvas product competing with mature WordPress/Elementor theme customizers, proving no substrate claim, on a target (shared hosting) where a second JS build is a liability. ARK already has the richest surface in the repo (21 templates [M]); a studio is open-ended and buys no authority.
  - **"Installation profiles as editions"** (doc item #5). Packaging and naming. The four `profile-*` modules already exist [M]; renaming them into editions is marketing overhead with zero user value and no buyer to sell an edition to.
  - **Search + SEO + navigation admin breadth, built on spec.** These are table stakes, not the wedge, and they are the correct *next* surfaces only once a real user asks. Building all three in parallel repeats the suite's existing mistake of breadth ahead of surface.
  - **More static-analysis work on the 94 unresolved non-HTTP call sites.** The doc already calls this the honest limit of the instrument [M]; the gate prevents growth; resolving it buys no user value and no revenue.
  - **Anything on the AIGovernance JSON store.** Do not extend a second governance system that duplicates and contradicts the capability bus. Migrate it onto the bus or delete it.
  - **P5 extension authority grants.** Keep deferred. There is not a single third-party extension in this repo [M]; building a grant market for a non-existent ecosystem is premature.
  - **`CompetitiveBenchmark`/competitive-gate expansion.** The substrate thesis explicitly says it is the wrong yardstick [M], and a benchmark runner exists anyway; more of it measures the wrong thing.

OVER_INVESTED:
  - **The React/Vite builder** (`cms-akira-builder/admin-ui`): 6 source files, 11 capabilities, 6 declared routes, and `node_modules` committed inside the module tree [M] — a second toolchain and second deploy artifact on a PHP/MySQL-5.7/Bluehost target, for a refusal ("no block-editor competition") the code contradicts.
  - **Governance instrumentation relative to users.** Workbench, the census, frozen baselines, `.governance-baseline.json`, 150+ `.ai/` contract/prompt/run-log files [M] — a very high measurement-to-measured ratio for a product with no verified tenant.
  - **The 15-module `cms-akira` suite.** Six modules have zero declared route authority and no user surface; the suite has zero `{ikb_entity_view}` contracts of its own; all 15 are disabled by default [M]. Breadth arrived before the surface that would consume it.
  - **Module-local tests that CI never runs.** `composer test` scans only `tests/`; module tests under `modules/cms-akira/*/tests/` are not executed by the main test job (only `shell_contract_test.php` is, explicitly) [M]. The suite *looks* well-tested and mostly isn't, which is a poor place to have invested and a worse place to keep spending.
  - **`daily-ledger`'s local governance** is under-invested, not over: it is untracked (0 tracked files [M]) yet it is the only live revenue-bearing domain and the only real second-domain evidence.

BIGGEST_RISK_YOU_SEE:
  The risk is not technical failure; it is that the programme keeps converting an unproven product thesis into more substrate, more instrumentation and more documentation while the only real revenue domain (`daily-ledger`) sits untracked and locally governed and the flagship AI differentiator is an `EchoAiProvider` with a cosmetic review badge. The direction doc's own ordering (admin surface → P3 → P4) is roughly right in shape, but its list of *other* items (Theme Studio, editions, Workbench console, search/SEO/nav breadth) is large enough that a solo owner plus AI implementers will spend the next several cycles on governed software nobody pays for. Bluehost/MySQL 5.7 plus one maintainer caps the supportable product, so breadth is not merely wasteful — it is the failure mode. The decision that actually matters is not "which feature next"; it is "who is the first paying user, and does Akira serve them, or does the kernel serve a vertical app they already run?" Until that is answered, every authority primitive is a beautifully measured liability.
