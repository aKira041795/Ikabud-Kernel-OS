WEDGE:
  position:      [INFERENCE] Test Akira as a shared-hostable, proof-carrying editorial approval system for small digital agencies publishing regulated client claims; this is a candidate wedge, not an established one.
  why_unoccupied:[INFERENCE] Contentful/Sanity place governance in higher-priced SaaS workflows; Strapi/Payload/Directus generally require a more capable hosting stack; WordPress fits shared hosting but its broadly privileged plugin/direct-database model makes a system-wide non-bypass claim difficult. None is technically incapable of reproducing the buyer outcome, however, so Akira has an underserved-price/package opportunity rather than a durable moat.
  buyer:         [INFERENCE] The buyer is the owner or operations director of a 5–20-person agency maintaining sites for credit unions, clinics or trade associations. Today they use WordPress, email/Teams approval, activity-log plugins and PDF or screenshot archives. The proposition is one inexpensive PHP deployment that makes draft-only automation, separate approval and portable evidence standard across client sites.
  confidence:    low   (No customer or pricing evidence exists in the repository. Two agencies paying for pilots involving real regulated content, plus a competitor benchmark showing materially lower deployment or audit cost, would raise it.)

SEQUENCE:
  1. Browser-proven editorial minimum
     claim:      [MEASURED] Route authority is genuinely enforced immediately before handler invocation in `src/helpers/module-manager.php`, and the current census reports Akira at 28/33 enforced with five undeclared writes. Demonstrate that enforcement, revision provenance and entity views remain usable through a complete browser journey.
     value:      An author can upload/select an image, create and edit a post, submit it, see its revision/actor timeline, and have an editor approve, publish or revert it without developer intervention.
     order_why:  Buyers cannot evaluate sophisticated delegation while the ordinary product remains incomplete, and PR #125 proved that API success does not establish browser usability. This iteration also removes ambiguity before machine authority is introduced.
     smallest:   Keep the existing `/cms-akira-shell/posts` and edit screens, add only `/cms-akira-shell/media`, and render `post.admin-list`, `post.admin-detail`, `post-revision.timeline` and `media.library` entity views through `akira.post.admin.list@1`, `akira.post.admin.get@1`, revision/revert capabilities and `akira.media.*@1`. Declare shell post-delete and both theme-activation routes; record reasoned exemptions for the two intentionally excluded `gui-settings` writes. Gate a real Playwright journey—login, upload, draft, submit, approve, publish, verify public page, revert—against MySQL 5.7. No bulk editor, search or visual-builder expansion.
  2. One bounded machine drafter
     claim:      P3 delegation is necessary because current caller-module/role policy cannot represent a machine grantee, grantor, expiry, subject scope or same-decision segregation. Demonstrate one `Actor`/`Grant`/`ExecutionContext` path at the existing capability boundary, not an AI-specific authority subsystem.
     value:      An operator sees exactly what one automation may do, can suspend or revoke it, and reviews its draft in a queue; the automation receives a typed denial if it attempts to publish.
     order_why:  It depends on the human editorial path being usable and browser-tested. Building proof export first would only attest to ordinary human operations, while adding more AI features first would create ungoverned breadth.
     smallest:   One machine actor; one tenant-scoped, expiring grant for `akira.post.create@1`/`akira.post.update@1` and suggestion generation; explicitly no `akira.post.publish@1` or publish transition. Add `/cms-akira-shell/automation` with `authority-grant.list/detail` views and `/cms-akira-shell/review` with filtered `draft-review.list/detail` views. Require a separately authenticated human to approve/publish and prove denial through HTTP and the actual service/queue entry point. One real provider and one draft operation are enough—no scheduling, fallback fleet or multi-agent orchestration.
  3. Portable proof for one published revision
     claim:      P4 can bind content, human and machine actors, grant, policy/declaration revision, audit correlation and publication capability into a signed artifact that an independent verifier checks without Akira or its database.
     value:      The agency can give a client or auditor a self-contained evidence bundle for the exact page that was published.
     order_why:  A proof containing machine delegation is the complete contender claim, so it must follow P3; it must not conceal missing enforcement behind cryptography.
     smallest:   A “Download proof” action on one published-post timeline producing canonical `artifact.json`, `proof.json` and a pinned public key/key identifier, plus an offline `verify-artifact()` command. Add a `publication-proof.detail` view. The verifier must reject changed content, actor, grant, policy version or signature after the bundle is copied away. Document that this proves integrity relative to the trusted key—not truth, independent timestamping or honest server operation.

GATED_AI:
  verdict:        depends
  argument:       [MEASURED] It is a demo thesis today. `cms-akira-ai` is a route-less, table-free deterministic extractive summariser over already-published `entity.get.post@1` data; it is not an AI actor or drafter. P3 and P4 are absent, five Akira writes remain undeclared, 94/95 non-HTTP call sites are unresolved, and Daily Ledger has 0/52 business operations on the capability path. Current workflow policy also allows an editor to approve and publish, so it does not enforce two-person segregation. [INFERENCE] A buyer may pay for reduced approval evidence and audit preparation, not for “responsible AI” language. Even completed, a signed bundle proves what Akira recorded and signed; it does not prove factual content, adequate human review, uncompromised signing keys or that an operator avoided Akira. The claim becomes undeniable only when a real machine is denied publication over every entry point, a distinct human approves, an independently held copy verifies against a previously trusted key, and a paying buyer uses that evidence in an actual review. It becomes a conference demo if the denial is prompt/UI convention, the same administrator controls both identities, generic non-HTTP paths remain unresolved, proof trusts a key delivered inside the same bundle, or the workflow is too cumbersome and users approve outside Akira.
  cheapest_test:  In one week, show five named agency/compliance prospects a clickable draft-review-proof flow, sample proof schema and explicit red-team acceptance contract, then ask each for a paid pilot using two real publications—e.g. a $1,500 setup deposit with an intended $300/month account. DO_NOT_BUILD:

  - Installation profiles marketed as editions: the four profile manifests contain no capabilities or surfaces; packaging incomplete behavior creates positioning, not value.
  - P5 extension-grant UX or a marketplace before a third-party extension and buyer demand exist; it is architecture in search of a customer.

  - A customer-facing Workbench console before customers ask for it; expose only the denial, grant and provenance information required by the editorial task.

FALSIFICATION:
  - Fewer than two of 10 qualified agency/compliance buyers pay the stated pilot deposit or provide real regulated publications after seeing the exact workflow—not a generic AI pitch. (cost: 1–2 founder weeks, under roughly $3,000 cash)
  - A neutral implementer configures WordPress or one named headless incumbent to pass the same draft-denial, separate-identity and offline-proof acceptance test at comparable total cost and within one working day. That would erase the proposed wedge. (cost: 1–2 engineer weeks plus roughly $1,000–$5,000 in trials/consulting)
  - A thin P3/P4 implementation cannot prevent a machine-originated publish through HTTP, service and queue paths without replacing the Bluehost/MySQL 5.7 deployment model or adding domain-specific bypasses. (cost: 3–5 engineer weeks for implementation and adversarial testing)
  - In a 30-day pilot, editors routinely share identities, approve outside Akira, export screenshots instead of proof bundles, or take longer than their current WordPress/email process with no audit-time reduction. (cost: one pilot, roughly 4–8 engineering weeks plus customer support)
  - Security reviews from five otherwise qualified buyers reject same-host control plane, commodity shared hosting or operator-held signing keys. That would invalidate the selected buyer/deployment pairing even if the software works. (cost: about one founder week)

BIGGEST_RISK_YOU_SEE:
  The largest risk is a category error: building a technically coherent proof system for buyers who merely want familiar approvals and an audit log. The repository proves significant authority machinery, but not demand, general non-bypassability or product usability: P3/P4 do not exist, Daily Ledger routes around the substrate, 94 non-HTTP sites remain unresolved, and browser coverage is not a mandatory gate. Worse, the likely governance buyer may reject Bluehost shared hosting precisely because they care about trust. Akira could therefore become unusually impressive infrastructure positioned between low-cost WordPress buyers who will not pay and regulated enterprises whose procurement requirements it cannot meet.
